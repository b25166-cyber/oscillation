🧠 Understand the NCERT concepts

Learn the ideas, then connect each one to the pendulum and graphs above.

🔁

Periodic motion

A motion that repeats itself at regular intervals of time is called periodic motion. Examples include the hands of a clock, Earth's rotation, and an ideal pendulum's repeated swings.

↔️

One complete oscillation

One complete oscillation means the object returns to its starting position moving in the same direction. For a pendulum starting at the left extreme: left → centre → right extreme → centre → left.

⏱️

Time period (T)

The time taken to complete one full cycle is the time period. Its SI unit is the second (s). In the small-angle model, a longer pendulum has a longer time period.

⚡

Frequency (f)

Frequency is the number of complete cycles per unit time. Its SI unit is hertz (Hz). One hertz means one cycle per second.

〰️

Periodic motion is not always SHM

All ideal SHM is periodic, but not every periodic motion is SHM. In SHM, acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed toward equilibrium.

📉

Read the graph

On a displacement-time graph, the time between matching points in consecutive cycles is T. The maximum displacement from equilibrium is the amplitude.

Key relationships
f = 1/T    and    T = 1/f
For ideal sinusoidal SHM, angular frequency and period are related by ω = 2π/T = 2πf, so T = 2π/ω. General periodic motion does not have to follow a sine or cosine curve.

🧪 NCERT-style worked examples

Try to solve each one yourself before opening the solution.

Example 1 — Find frequency and time period

A human heart beats 75 times in 60 seconds. Find its average frequency and time period.

Step 1: Frequency
f = number of beats / time = 75 / 60 = 1.25 Hz
Step 2: Time period
T = 1/f = 1/1.25 = 0.8 s
Answer: f = 1.25 Hz and T = 0.8 s.
Example 2 — Count the cycles

An object completes 20 oscillations in 10 seconds. Find f and T.

f = 20/10 = 2 Hz
T = 1/f = 1/2 = 0.5 s
Answer: frequency = 2 Hz and time period = 0.5 s.
Example 3 — Pendulum length and time period

Estimate the time period of a 1.00 m pendulum using g = 9.8 m/s².

T = 2π√(L/g)
T = 2π√(1.00/9.8)
T ≈ 2.01 s
Note: this is the small-angle approximation.
