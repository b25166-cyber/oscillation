🧠 Learn the Concepts

Understand the physical idea, then connect it to the mathematical model.

What are damped oscillations?

Oscillations whose amplitude decreases with time because resistive forces remove mechanical energy from the oscillator.

Amplitude decreases with time

What causes damping?

Friction, air resistance or resistance from a fluid transfers mechanical energy to the surroundings, often as heat.

Fd = −bv

Meaning of b

The damping coefficient describes the strength of the resistive force in this model. Larger b generally makes the motion die away faster.

Fd ∝ −v

Natural angular frequency

The angular frequency the ideal spring–mass oscillator would have without damping.

ω₀ = √(k/m)

Damped angular frequency

When damping is not too strong, the oscillator still crosses equilibrium repeatedly, usually at a lower angular frequency.

ω′ = √(ω₀² − b²/4m²)

What happens to energy?

Mechanical energy decreases as energy is transferred to the surroundings. Total energy of the larger system is still conserved.

E ≈ ½kA²

📘 Equations You Should Know

These equations describe a spring–mass oscillator with linear viscous damping.

m x″ + b x′ + kx = 0

Equation of motion: mass × acceleration, damping force and spring force sum to zero.

Fd = −bv

The damping force acts opposite to velocity in the viscous-damping model.

ω₀ = √(k/m)

Natural angular frequency without damping, in rad/s.

x(t) = A₀e−bt/(2m) cos(ω′t + φ)

Common form of underdamped displacement; phase φ depends on initial conditions.

A(t) = A₀e−bt/(2m)

Exponential amplitude envelope for underdamped motion.

ω′ = √(k/m − b²/4m²)

Damped angular frequency when b² < 4mk.

E(t) ≈ ½kA₀²e−bt/m

Energy-envelope approximation for weak damping, averaged over an oscillation.

T′ = 2π/ω′

Period of the underdamped oscillation.

f′ = ω′/(2π)

Damped frequency in hertz.

🌍 Where Do We See Damping?

Connect the model to familiar situations.

🚗 Vehicle suspension

Shock absorbers reduce bouncing after a vehicle passes over a bump.

🚪 Door closer

Resistance helps a door return smoothly rather than swinging repeatedly.

🎸 Vibrating strings

A plucked string loses energy, so its vibration and sound gradually fade.

🧮 Worked Examples

Try each question before opening the solution.

Example 1: Amplitude decay time

A mass m = 1 kg has damping coefficient b = 2 kg/s. How long does the amplitude take to fall to 1/e of its initial value?

Show solution
A(t) = A₀e−bt/(2m). For A/A₀ = 1/e, bt/(2m) = 1.
t = 2m/b = 2 × 1/2
Answer: 1 s

Example 2: Natural angular frequency

A 1 kg mass is attached to a spring of stiffness 25 N/m. Find its undamped natural angular frequency.

Show solution
ω₀ = √(k/m) = √(25/1)
Answer: 5 rad/s

Example 3: Critical damping coefficient

A system has mass 1 kg and spring stiffness 16 N/m. Find bc.

Show solution
bc = 2√(mk) = 2√(1 × 16)
Answer: 8 kg/s
