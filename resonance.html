01 · The big idea

What is resonance?

Every oscillator has a natural frequency — the frequency at which it tends to oscillate when disturbed and then allowed to move freely.

A driving force is an external force that repeatedly pushes or pulls a system. The frequency of this force is called the driving frequency.

Resonance: driving frequency ≈ natural frequency

When the driving frequency matches or is very close to the natural frequency, the oscillator can respond with a large amplitude. This phenomenon is called resonance.

Remember: resonance is not simply “moving fast.” It is a particularly large response caused by the relationship between the driving frequency and the system's natural frequency.

03 · Understand the physics

Free, damped and forced oscillations

Free

Free oscillations

After an initial disturbance, a system oscillates under its own restoring force at its natural frequency. In the ideal case, its amplitude stays constant.

Damped

Damped oscillations

Resistive forces, such as friction or air resistance, dissipate mechanical energy. The amplitude decreases with time.

Driven

Forced oscillations

An external periodic force keeps supplying energy to the system. After the initial transient dies away, the steady motion follows the driving frequency.

04 · Important formulas

The equations behind resonance

Natural frequency of a spring–mass system

ω₀ = √(k/m)

Here, k is the spring constant and m is the mass. The natural frequency in hertz is

f₀ = ω₀ / 2π

Driven, damped oscillator

mẍ + bẋ + kx = F₀ cos(ωd t)

m = mass, b = damping coefficient, k = spring constant, F₀ = driving-force amplitude and ωd = driving angular frequency.

The external force continuously supplies energy while damping removes it.

Steady-state amplitude

A = F₀ / √[(k − mωd²)² + (bωd)²]

This formula shows that the amplitude depends on driving frequency and damping. Near resonance, the response can become large.

What damping changes

  • Greater damping reduces the resonance peak.
  • Lower damping produces a taller, sharper peak.
  • Real systems have finite amplitudes because of damping and other limits.

Note: ω₀ is the undamped natural angular frequency. With damping, the exact frequency of maximum displacement response can differ slightly from ω₀; in the lightly damped case, resonance is close to ωd = ω₀.

05 · Everyday physics

Where do we see resonance?

🛝 A swing

Push a swing in rhythm with its natural motion. Each well-timed push adds energy, helping the swing reach a greater height.

🏗️ Bridges and buildings

Wind, machinery or earthquake motion can drive a structure. If the driving frequency is close to one of its natural frequencies, vibrations may become large.

🎸 Musical instruments

Vibrations can excite resonant parts of an instrument, helping particular frequencies produce a stronger sound.

🎵 Sound and air columns

An air column can respond strongly at its resonant frequencies. This helps explain how many wind instruments produce sound.

Safety connection: resonance can be useful, but engineers must account for it in structures and machines to avoid excessive vibration. Soldiers may break step when crossing a bridge to avoid repeatedly applying a synchronized periodic force.
